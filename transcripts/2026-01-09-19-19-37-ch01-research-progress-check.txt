      "content": "# Knowledge Embedding Technical Routes: A Comprehensive Landscape\n\nKnowledge can be embedded into large language models through five distinct technical routes: continued pre-training, supervised fine-tuning, parameter-efficient fine-tuning, model editing, and prompt caching\u2014each offering fundamentally different tradeoffs between permanence, efficiency, and flexibility. RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) operates as an orthogonal approach that retrieves rather than embeds knowledge, making these techniques complementary rather than competing. This survey provides a systematic analysis of each route with academic references, core innovations, and comparative positioning against RAG-based approaches.\n\n---\n\n## 1. Continued pre-training adapts models to domains and tasks\n\nContinued pre-training extends a model's initial training phase on domain-specific or task-specific corpora, embedding new knowledge directly into model parameters before downstream fine-tuning.\n\n### Don't Stop Pretraining (Gururangan et al., ACL 2020)\n\nThis foundational work established the effectiveness of multi-phase adaptive pre-training for domain specialization.\n\n| Attribute | Details |\n|-----------|---------|\n| **Full Title** | Don't Stop Pretraining: Adapt Language Models to Domains and Tasks |\n| **Links** | arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.10964 \u2022 ACL Anthology: https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.740/ |\n| **Venue** | ACL 2020 (Honorable Mention for Best Paper) |\n| **Core Innovation** | Introduces **DAPT** (Domain-Adaptive Pre-training) on large domain corpora and **TAPT** (Task-Adaptive Pre-training) on unlabeled task data. TAPT achieves comparable gains to DAPT while being **60\u00d7 faster** and using **5.8 million times less storage**. |\n| **vs. RAG** | Embeds domain expertise in parameters (no retrieval overhead), but requires retraining to update and risks catastrophic forgetting |\n\n### SSR: Self-Synthesized Rehearsal (Huang et al., ACL 2024)\n\n| Attribute | Details |\n|-----------|---------|\n| **Full Title** | Mitigating Catastrophic Forgetting in Large Language Models with Self-Synthesized Rehearsal |\n| **Links** | arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.01244 \u2022 ACL Anthology: https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.77/ |\n| **Venue** | ACL 2024 |\n| **Core Innovation** | Addresses continual learning **when original training data is unavailable** (e.g., for released checkpoints like Llama-2). Uses LLM-generated synthetic instances with k-means diversity selection for rehearsal, requiring only **1% real data** for ICL demonstrations. |\n| **vs. RAG** | Internalizes knowledge via synthetic replay without external database; preferable when inference latency matters |\n\n### Catastrophic forgetting remains the central challenge\n\nThe **catastrophic forgetting** problem\u2014where learning new information degrades performance on previously learned tasks\u2014motivates most continued pre-training research. Key mitigation strategies include:\n\n| Strategy | Key Paper | Venue | Core Approach |\n|----------|-----------|-------|---------------|\n| **Elastic Weight Consolidation** | Kirkpatrick et al. | PNAS 2017 | Fisher Information Matrix identifies critical parameters; quadratic penalty prevents excessive updates |\n| **Implicit Inference View** | Kotha et al. | ICLR 2024 | Forgetting reframed as task inference drift; \"Conjugate Prompting\" recovers pretrained capabilities |\n| **Experience Replay** | Rolnick et al. | NeurIPS 2019 | Maintains replay buffer of past examples; recent work (arXiv:2508.01908) shows small replay rates outperform scaling model size |\n\nThe comprehensive survey \"Continual Learning of Large Language Models\" (ACM Computing Surveys, 2025; GitHub: https://github.com/Wang-ML-Lab/llm-continual-learning-survey) provides systematic coverage of these approaches.\n\n---\n\n## 2. Supervised fine-tuning teaches style rather than knowledge\n\nThe Superficial Alignment Hypothesis, first proposed in the **LIMA paper**, fundamentally reframes our understanding of what supervised fine-tuning accomplishes.\n\n### LIMA demonstrates quality over quantity (Zhou et al., NeurIPS 2023)\n\n| Attribute | Details |\n|-----------|---------|\n| **Full Title** | LIMA: Less Is More for Alignment |\n| **Links** | arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11206 \u2022 NeurIPS: https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/ac662d74829e4407ce1d126477f4a03a-Abstract-Conference.html |\n| **Venue** | NeurIPS 2023 |\n| **Core Innovation** | Fine-tuning on just **1,000 carefully curated examples** matches models trained on 52,000+ examples or with RLHF. Proposes the **Superficial Alignment Hypothesis**: \"A model's knowledge and capabilities are learned almost entirely during pretraining; alignment teaches it which subdistribution of formats should be used.\" |\n| **vs. RAG** | SFT primarily elicits existing knowledge in preferred formats rather than adding new knowledge; RAG genuinely injects new factual information |\n\nDirect empirical validation came from **\"The Unlocking Spell on Base LLMs\"** (Lin et al., ICLR 2024; arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.01552), which demonstrated that **92.2% of tokens** generated by aligned models are within the top-3 tokens ranked by base models\u2014shifts occur predominantly in stylistic tokens, not content.\n\n### Instruction tuning enables zero-shot generalization\n\n| Paper | Venue | Link | Core Finding |\n|-------|-------|------|--------------|\n| **FLAN** (Wei et al.) | ICLR 2022 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652 | Instruction tuning on 60+ tasks enables zero-shot generalization; FLAN-137B surpassed GPT-3 175B on 20/25 tasks |\n| **Instruction Tuning Survey** (Zhang et al.) | ACM Computing Surveys 2025 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10792 | Comprehensive methodology review; GitHub: https://github.com/xiaoya-li/Instruction-Tuning-Survey |\n| **Fine-Tuning vs. RAG** (Ovadia et al.) | EMNLP 2024 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.05934 | RAG consistently outperforms fine-tuning for knowledge injection; LLMs struggle to learn new facts through fine-tuning |\n\n---\n\n## 3. Parameter-efficient methods reduce training costs dramatically\n\nPEFT methods modify only a small fraction of model parameters, enabling fine-tuning of billion-parameter models on consumer hardware while preserving most pre-trained knowledge.\n\n### The LoRA family dominates modern practice\n\n| Method | Year/Venue | Link | Core Innovation | Parameter Overhead |\n|--------|------------|------|-----------------|-------------------|\n| **LoRA** (Hu et al.) | ICLR 2022 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685 | Low-rank decomposition \u0394W = BA where r \u226a min(d,k); **no inference latency** (weights merge) | ~0.1% |\n| **QLoRA** (Dettmers et al.) | NeurIPS 2023 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14314 | **4-bit NormalFloat** quantization + LoRA; enables **65B model on single 48GB GPU** | ~0.1% + quantization |\n| **DoRA** (Liu et al.) | ICML 2024 (Oral) | https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09353 | Decomposes weights into **magnitude and direction**; LoRA for direction, separate magnitude vector; better matches full fine-tuning dynamics | ~0.1% |\n| **QDoRA** (Answer.AI) | Technical Report 2024 | https://github.com/AnswerDotAI/fsdp_qlora | 4-bit quantization + DoRA + FSDP; **outperforms full fine-tuning by 5%** on Orca-Math while using 9\u00d7 less memory | ~0.1% |\n\n**Adapters** (Houlsby et al., ICML 2019; arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.00751) pioneered PEFT by inserting bottleneck layers between transformer blocks. Unlike LoRA, adapters **add inference latency** but provide stronger modularity for continual learning.\n\n### PEFT vs. RAG positioning\n\nPEFT excels at teaching models *how* to respond (style, format, domain behavior) while RAG provides *what* information to use. A 2024 study (arXiv:2409.09510) found RAG yielded **14.92% improvement** on knowledge-intensive personalization tasks versus just **1.07%** for PEFT methods\u2014confirming that RAG is more effective for pure knowledge embedding while PEFT is superior for behavioral adaptation.\n\n---\n\n## 4. Model editing enables surgical knowledge modification\n\nModel editing directly modifies specific parameters to update, correct, or delete factual associations without full retraining, based on the theoretical insight that **MLP layers function as key-value memories**.\n\n### Locating knowledge in transformers\n\nThe theoretical foundation for model editing rests on two key findings:\n\n**\"Transformer Feed-Forward Layers Are Key-Value Memories\"** (Geva et al., EMNLP 2021; arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.14913) demonstrated that FFN layers operate as associative memories where keys (first linear layer) correlate with textual patterns and values (second linear layer) induce vocabulary distributions.\n\n**\"Knowledge Neurons in Pretrained Transformers\"** (Dai et al., ACL 2022; arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08696) introduced the knowledge neurons thesis\u2014specific neurons store specific facts, identifiable via integrated gradients attribution.\n\n### ROME and MEMIT enable precise factual editing\n\n| Method | Year/Venue | Link | Core Innovation | Scale |\n|--------|------------|------|-----------------|-------|\n| **ROME** (Meng et al.) | NeurIPS 2022 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05262 | **Causal tracing** identifies decisive activations; **rank-one modification** writes new associations to single MLP layer | Single facts |\n| **MEMIT** (Meng et al.) | ICLR 2023 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07229 | Distributes updates across **multiple critical MLP layers**; knowledge-sharding prevents conflicts | Lifelong editing |\n\n**EasyEdit** (Zhang et al., ACL 2024 Demo; GitHub: https://github.com/zjunlp/EasyEdit) provides a unified framework implementing ROME, MEMIT, WISE, MEND, SERAC, GRACE, and other methods across T5, GPT, LLaMA, Mistral, and Qwen architectures.\n\n### Model editing vs. RAG: fundamentally different mechanisms\n\nModel editing modifies **intrinsic parametric knowledge**\u2014edited facts become \"native\" and generalize to paraphrases. RAG augments with **extrinsic knowledge** at inference\u2014the model's internal knowledge remains unchanged. Model editing suits permanent corrections without retrieval overhead; RAG suits dynamic, traceable, easily updateable knowledge with citations.\n\n---\n\n## 5. In RAG pipelines, caching the retrieved context eliminates redundant processing when the same documents are queried multiple times. The relationship is synergistic: RAG retrieves relevant knowledge dynamically, while caching reduces the per-turn cost of processing that retrieved context.\n\n---\n\n## Comparative positioning across all five routes\n\n| Dimension | Continued Pre-training | SFT | PEFT | Model Editing | Prompt Caching |\n|-----------|----------------------|-----|------|---------------|----------------|\n| **Knowledge Type** | Domain patterns, linguistic expertise | Style, format, task behavior | Behavioral adaptation | Specific factual corrections | Repeated context |\n| **Update Mechanism** | Full retraining | Fine-tuning | Adapter/LoRA training | Direct parameter modification | Cache refresh |\n| **Forgetting Risk** | High | Moderate | Low | Localized | None |\n| **Inference Overhead** | None | None | None (LoRA) / Small (Adapters) | None | Reduced |\n| **vs. RAG** | Deep expertise vs. dynamic retrieval | Elicits format vs. injects facts | Adapts behavior vs. provides knowledge | Permanent edits vs. updateable DB | Complements RAG |\n\n**The fundamental insight**: These five routes modify *how the model behaves* or *what it knows parametrically*, while RAG provides *external knowledge at inference*.
